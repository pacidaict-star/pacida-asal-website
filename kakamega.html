Kakamega County briefing

Kenya · area 3,034 km² · density 616 / km² · avg household 4.3 persons

Kakamega is the dense, green heartland of Luhya-land in western Kenya, one of the country's most densely populated rural counties and historically its sugar bowl. Smallholder sugarcane, maize and finger-millet plots cover almost every acre outside Kakamega Forest — the last surviving remnant of the rainforest that once stretched across equatorial Africa, now a biodiversity and eco-tourism anchor. The collapse of Mumias Sugar Company through the 2010s hollowed out the formal cash economy, pushing households toward boda boda transport, small trade and casual labour. Rainfall is reliable (1,200–1,800 mm) but land fragmentation, seasonal flooding along the Nzoia and Yala rivers, and high youth unemployment are the county's defining development pressures.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Agriculture & the sugar economy
Smallholder sugarcane remains the backbone of household income alongside maize, beans and finger millet, but the near-collapse of Mumias Sugar Company left tens of thousands of out-growers without a reliable buyer, forcing diversification into horticulture, dairy and boda boda transport. Land parcels have fragmented to under an acre in many areas, squeezing yields.
Kakamega Forest & tourism
Kakamega Forest, the easternmost outlier of the Guineo-Congolian rainforest system, anchors a growing eco-tourism and research economy (birding, primates, butterflies) around Isecheno and Buyangu, run jointly with KWS and the Kenya Forest Service. Encroachment for firewood and farmland remains the core conservation pressure.
Land pressure & livelihoods
Among Kenya's most densely settled rural counties, average landholding sizes have fallen below one acre in several sub-counties, driving distress land sales, rural-urban migration to Nairobi and the Gulf, and a large informal boda boda and jua kali sector absorbing under-employed youth.
Health, water & flooding
Seasonal flooding of the Nzoia and Yala rivers displaces riverine households most rainy seasons, especially in Navakholo and Matungu; malaria remains holoendemic given the warm, humid climate, and piped water coverage lags rural demand outside Kakamega town.

Climate risk context

Kakamega County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

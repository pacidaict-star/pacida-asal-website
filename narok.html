Narok County briefing

Kenya · area 17,921.2 km² · density 65 / km² · avg household 4.6 persons

Narok stretches from the wheat-covered Mau plains in the north to the acacia savannah of the Maasai Mara in the south, Kenya's premier wildlife-tourism county and a Maasai pastoral heartland. Cattle, sheep and goats remain central to Maasai livelihoods and culture, while community conservancies bordering the Maasai Mara National Reserve generate some of the country's largest wildlife-tourism revenues. Mechanised wheat and barley farming on the Mau/Narok plains adds a substantial commercial-agriculture dimension rare among ASAL counties. The Mau Forest Complex, source of the Mara River, is the county's critical — and increasingly threatened — water tower. Narok's southern and eastern pastoral rangelands carry NDMA/ASAL classification, distinguishing them from the wetter, higher-potential north.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 9–15% in Narok South/Trans Mara pastoral wards during drought years; lower in the wheat-farming north

Livestock & pastoral economy
Cattle remain central to Maasai identity and wealth across Narok South, West and Trans Mara; small-stock (sheep/goats) provide a faster-liquidating buffer in drought years. Herds move seasonally between the Loita hills, Mau forest fringe and Mara plains as pasture and water allow.
Wildlife tourism & conservancies
The Maasai Mara National Reserve and adjoining community conservancies (Mara Naboisho, Olare Motorogi, Ol Kinyei) are one of Kenya's top foreign-exchange earners and a major source of land-lease income and employment for Maasai landowners, though benefit-sharing and human-wildlife conflict remain live issues.
Mechanised agriculture
The Mau/Narok plains around Narok North and Narok East host some of Kenya's largest mechanised wheat and barley farms, making the county a significant national grain producer alongside its pastoral economy.
Water, forest & environment
The Mau Forest Complex, a critical water tower feeding the Mara River and Lake Victoria basin, has suffered significant deforestation from settlement and logging; resulting siltation and reduced dry-season flows threaten both the Mara ecosystem's tourism value and downstream pastoral water access.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

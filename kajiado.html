Kajiado County briefing

Kenya · area 21,293 km² · density 52 / km² · avg household 3.6 persons

Kajiado is Kenya's fastest-urbanising ASAL county, its northern wards — Ongata Rongai, Kiserian, Kitengela, Isinya — now dense Nairobi commuter suburbs sitting inside what remains, on paper, classic Maasai rangeland. Drive an hour south, however, and the county reverts to the pastoralism, dispersed manyattas and open grassland of Kajiado Central, West and South, home to Amboseli National Park's iconic Mt Kilimanjaro backdrop and the Magadi soda-ash works on the shores of Lake Magadi. Land subdivision, wildlife conservancies and urban sprawl are eroding traditional grazing commons faster than in almost any other Maasai county, even as tourism and Nairobi-adjacent real estate give Kajiado significantly lower poverty than its ASAL neighbours further north.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 7–12% in drought years (Loitokitok/Magadi highest)

Livestock & rangelands
Maasai cattle and shoat pastoralism remains the backbone of Kajiado Central, West and South's rural economy, though land subdivision, fencing and the loss of communal grazing corridors are steadily reducing herd mobility and drought resilience compared to a generation ago. Drought years push herders into conservancies and neighbouring counties in search of pasture.
Nairobi-adjacent urbanisation
Ongata Rongai, Kiserian, Kitengela and Isinya have become some of Kenya's fastest-growing commuter towns, absorbing Nairobi's overflow population and transforming Kajiado North and East from pastoral rangeland into dense residential and light-industrial suburbs within little more than two decades — a land-use shift with few parallels elsewhere in ASAL Kenya.
Tourism & conservation
Amboseli National Park, its Mt Kilimanjaro backdrop and a dense network of community wildlife conservancies in Kajiado South generate significant tourism revenue and conservancy-lease income for Maasai landowners, though human-wildlife conflict and competition between grazing and wildlife land use remain persistent friction points, especially during drought, when both livestock and wildlife concentrate around the same shrinking water points.
Water & industry
Kajiado West and South face recurring dry-season water stress despite the county's proximity to Nairobi's own water infrastructure; the Magadi soda-ash works on Lake Magadi's shores is a major industrial employer and revenue source that itself depends on a fragile, highly saline lake ecosystem sensitive to rainfall variability.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

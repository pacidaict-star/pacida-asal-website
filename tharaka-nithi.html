Tharaka-Nithi County briefing

Kenya · area 2,662 km² · density 148 / km² · avg household 3.7 persons

Tharaka-Nithi is a county of two climates on the eastern slopes of Mt Kenya. Chuka, Maara and Igambang'ombe sit in fertile, well-watered mid-highlands where tea, coffee, miraa and bananas thrive — among the most productive smallholder land in eastern Kenya. Cross the Thanantu escarpment north-east, however, and the land drops onto the hot, dry Tharaka plains, semi-arid bushland classified ASAL where sorghum, millet, goats and honey are the mainstay and drought is a recurring shock. The Thuci, Mutonga and Thingithu rivers rise in these highlands, feeding the Kiambere hydropower dam downstream. Miraa is a major cash earner region-wide, tying local incomes to volatile export demand.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 7–12% (Tharaka North & South lowlands; Chuka/Maara highlands not a hotspot)

Highland–lowland duality
Chuka and Maara's reliable rainfall (1,200mm+) supports intensive tea and coffee smallholdings, while Tharaka North and South, under 700mm of erratic rain, are classified ASAL with sorghum, millet, goats and honey the norm. Development planning here has to run two completely different playbooks.
Miraa economy
Miraa (khat) is a major cash crop bridging both zones, feeding the Maua and onward Nairobi/export markets. Household incomes tied to miraa are volatile, swinging with export-market access (notably to Somalia) and periodic bans in destination countries.
Water & drought (Tharaka)
The Thuci and Mutonga rivers and sand dams are the lifeline for Tharaka North and South; dry-season trekking distances there routinely exceed the highland norm. The contrast with Chuka and Maara's reliable springs is the county's starkest infrastructure gap.
Health & nutrition
GAM concentrates heavily in Tharaka's lowland wards while highland Chuka/Maara children track close to the national average. The Kiambere hydropower dam downstream benefits the national grid far more than the local Tharaka communities living beside it.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

Taita Taveta County briefing

Kenya · area 17,084 km² · density 20 / km² · avg household 3.4 persons

Taita Taveta straddles the coastal hinterland between Mombasa and the Rift Valley, dominated by the misty Taita Hills and the vast plains of Tsavo East and Tsavo West national parks, which together cover over half the county's land. Voi, on the Nairobi–Mombasa highway and railway, is the commercial hub, while Wundanyi sits cooler in the hills. Livelihoods split sharply: intensive horticulture and dairy in the Taita Hills' fertile micro-climates versus semi-arid ranching, sisal estates and wildlife-based tourism on the surrounding lowlands. Human-wildlife conflict along the Tsavo corridors is a defining management challenge, alongside chronic water scarcity outside the hills, and small-scale gemstone mining (tsavorite, tourmaline) is a growing informal income source.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 6–11% (Voi and Sagalla lowlands highest in dry years)

Wildlife economy & human-wildlife conflict
Tsavo East and West cover more than 60% of the county's land, anchoring a major tourism economy but also producing chronic crop-raiding and livestock predation along the Mwatate–Rukanga and Sagalla corridors. KWS compensation schemes and community conservancies are the main mitigation tools, though payouts routinely lag losses.
Highland agriculture
The Taita Hills' fertile, high-rainfall micro-climate supports intensive horticulture (tomatoes, bananas, a fast-growing avocado export trade) and dairy, but extreme land fragmentation and steep-slope farming drive soil erosion. Yields here are among the highest per hectare in the wider coastal region.
Water & irrigation
Lowland Voi and Mwatate depend on the seasonal Voi and Mwatate rivers, boreholes and water pans for people and livestock; Taveta draws on the more reliable Lumi River and Lake Chala springs, supporting irrigated maize and rice. WASH coverage drops sharply outside the hills.
Livelihoods & poverty
Declining sisal estates, once the county's largest formal employer, have left youth unemployment concentrated in Voi and Taveta towns. Small-scale gemstone mining (tsavorite, tourmaline, ruby) around Mwatate and Voi supplements pastoral and farming incomes but remains largely informal and unregulated.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

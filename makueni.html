Makueni County briefing

Kenya · area 8,009 km² · density 123 / km² · avg household 3.9 persons

One of the three Ukambani counties and a national pioneer in community-led drought resilience: Makueni has built more sand dams — over 1,000 — than any other county in Africa, harvesting seasonal river flow to secure water and recharge groundwater through the dry months. Maize and legume farming is rain-fed and precarious across most of the county, but Kibwezi's irrigation schemes and the county-backed Kalamba fruit-processing plant have helped make mango and orange growing a genuine cash crop. Sand harvesting along the Kaiti and other seasonal rivers is a major, if environmentally contentious, livelihood. Wote, the county HQ, sits on the main Nairobi–Mombasa corridor, and Kibwezi's Tsavo West and Chyulu Hills frontier brings a modest tourism economy alongside the pastoral fringe.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 6–12% (Kibwezi East/West highest in drought years)

Water harvesting & sand dams
Makueni has built more sand dams than any other county in Kenya — well over 1,000 — trapping seasonal river flow behind small concrete walls to recharge groundwater and secure dry-season water for households, livestock and small-scale irrigation. The model, pioneered with NGOs like the Africa Sand Dam Foundation, is now studied nationally and regionally as a low-cost drought-resilience technology.
Rain-fed agriculture & drought risk
Maize, beans, pigeon peas, sorghum and millet are grown across most of the county on rain-fed plots highly exposed to the two-season rainfall pattern's frequent failures. Fruit farming — mangoes and oranges especially — has expanded fast, supported by the county-backed Kalamba fruit-processing plant, giving households a more drought-tolerant cash crop alongside traditional staples.
Livestock & sand harvesting
Goats and some cattle are kept across the drier eastern wards (Kibwezi, Kathonzweni), providing a buffer when crops fail. Commercial sand harvesting along the Kaiti and other seasonal riverbeds is a major, if environmentally contentious, source of casual income and county revenue, with over-extraction blamed for river-bed degradation and water-table decline in places.
Tourism & the Tsavo/Chyulu frontier
Kibwezi's proximity to Tsavo West National Park and the Chyulu Hills brings a modest but growing tourism and conservancy economy, alongside the Nairobi–Mombasa highway trade that flows through Mtito Andei and Makindu. Wildlife corridors and human-wildlife conflict management are an increasing county planning concern as settlement expands toward the park boundary.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

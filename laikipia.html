Laikipia County briefing

Kenya · area 8,696 km² · density 60 / km² · avg household 3.5 persons

Laikipia sprawls across the high plateau between Mt Kenya and the Rift Valley escarpment, a mosaic of large-scale wheat and barley farms, cattle ranches and some of Africa's most celebrated wildlife conservancies — Ol Pejeta (a rhino sanctuary) and Lewa (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) both sit within its borders. Nanyuki, the county's commercial hub at Mt Kenya's northern foot, has grown fast on tourism, flower export and a British Army training presence. The south and centre are high-potential agricultural land, but the northern rangelands around Dol Dol, Mukogodo and Il Ngwesi are true ASAL, home to Maasai and Samburu pastoralists whose herds compete with wildlife for dry-season grazing and water drawn from the Ewaso Ng'iro headwaters that downstream Isiolo and Samburu depend on.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 6–11% in Laikipia North (Mukogodo/Il Ngwesi); not a hotspot elsewhere in the county

Agriculture & large-scale ranching
Commercial wheat, barley and seed-potato farms and large cattle ranches (many overlapping wildlife conservancies) dominate the county's centre and west. Laikipia is one of Kenya's most productive dryland-agriculture zones, but rainfall is erratic and land-use planning between ranching, farming and conservancy interests is a constant negotiation, especially during dry years when grazing pressure spikes.
Wildlife conservation & tourism
Ol Pejeta Conservancy (East Africa's largest black rhino sanctuary and home to the world's last northern white rhinos) and the UNESCO-listed Lewa Wildlife Conservancy anchor a globally significant conservation economy. Community and private conservancies cover roughly a third of the county, generating tourism revenue and conservation jobs, though human-wildlife conflict rises sharply whenever drought pushes wildlife and livestock onto the same shrinking pastures.
Water & the Ewaso Ng'iro headwaters
Laikipia sits astride the headwaters of the Ewaso Ng'iro river system that Isiolo, Samburu and Garissa depend on downstream. Upstream irrigation and commercial abstraction by farms and flower growers have repeatedly reduced dry-season flow, a persistent source of tension with downstream pastoralist counties and the subject of ongoing water-allocation negotiations facilitated by the Ewaso Ng'iro Water Resource Users Association.
Northern pastoral drylands & conflict
Laikipia North's Mukogodo forest and community conservancies (Il Ngwesi, Naibunga) are Maasai and Samburu pastoral country, the county's only NDMA-monitored ASAL belt. Drought years push herds onto neighbouring ranches and conservancies, occasionally triggering land invasions and cattle-rustling episodes that have periodically made national headlines; peace committees and grazing agreements between ranch owners and pastoralist communities are the main mitigation tool.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

Baringo County briefing

Kenya · area 11,015 km² · density 61 / km² · avg household 4.8 persons

Baringo straddles Kenya's Rift Valley floor and the Tugen Hills escarpment, running from Lake Bogoria's flamingo-lined shores and the hot springs at Loboi to the arid plains of Tiaty in the north-east. Around county HQ Kabarnet and the Eldama Ravine highlands, smallholder farmers grow maize, wheat and dairy on fertile volcanic soils; drop into the Kerio Valley or East Pokot and the economy turns pastoral, cattle- and goat-based, and chronically exposed to drought and cross-border banditry with neighbouring West Pokot. Geothermal exploration around Lake Bogoria and the Perkerra irrigation scheme near Marigat hint at untapped potential in a county that is, geographically, two very different Kenyas within one boundary.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 9–15% in drought years (Tiaty highest)

Livestock & rangelands
Cattle and goats dominate lowland Baringo South and Tiaty, where East Pokot pastoralists depend on the Kerio Valley's sparse grazing and seasonal migration to the Tugen Hills escarpment during drought. Kabarnet and Eldama Ravine livestock markets link herders to Nakuru and Eldoret trade routes. Recurrent dry spells and shrinking communal grazing land, compounded by charcoal-driven deforestation, are steadily reducing carrying capacity across the county's pastoral wards.
Water, irrigation & the Rift lakes
The Perkerra irrigation scheme near Marigat supplies onions and horticulture from the Perkerra River, while Lake Baringo and Lake Bogoria anchor fishing, tourism and geothermal exploration interest. Elsewhere, boreholes, water pans and the seasonal Kerio River are the main sources; Tiaty's dispersed pastoral settlements face the county's longest dry-season trekking distances for both people and herds.
Nutrition, drought & security
East Pokot (Tiaty) periodically registers malnutrition and food-insecurity spikes comparable to classic ASAL counties further north, driven by drought and chronic insecurity. Cattle-rustling and banditry along the Kerio Valley and Baringo–Laikipia axis repeatedly disrupt markets, displace households and restrict humanitarian access, making conflict as significant a driver of vulnerability here as rainfall failure.
Highland agriculture & tourism potential
Kabarnet, Eldama Ravine and the Tugen Hills support maize, wheat, dairy and increasingly avocado farming on fertile volcanic soils, giving the county a genuine high-potential agricultural core alongside its dryland half. Lake Bogoria's flamingos, hot springs and geothermal prospects, plus growing honey and beekeeping enterprises, represent under-exploited diversification opportunities for a county straddling two very different economies.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

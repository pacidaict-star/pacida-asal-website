Mombasa County briefing

Kenya · area 295 km² · density 4,096 / km² · avg household 2.9 persons

Kenya's second city and only true island-and-coast urban county — a compact 295 km² holding over a million people at some of the highest densities in the country. Mombasa's Kilindini port handles the overwhelming majority of Kenya's and much of East Africa's maritime trade, and the county's economy runs on logistics, oil and gas handling, tourism (Fort Jesus, Old Town, Nyali and beaches toward Diani) and a large informal trade sector. Likoni's ferry crossing, one of Africa's busiest, and the newer Likoni floating footbridge symbolise the county's chronic transport bottlenecks between the island CBD and its southern mainland suburbs. Coastal erosion, flooding in low-lying Kisauni and Likoni settlements, and heat stress are the county's most pressing climate concerns.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Sector deep-dive click a heading to expand

Port & maritime economy
Kilindini Port handles the large majority of Kenya's seaborne trade and much of landlocked East Africa's transit cargo, anchoring shipping, logistics, freight-forwarding and oil-handling industries that dominate Mombasa's formal economy. The SGR's Mombasa terminus and ongoing port-expansion projects (second container terminal, dry ports inland) are the county's biggest single economic driver.
Tourism & heritage
Fort Jesus and the Old Town's Swahili-Arab streets, alongside Nyali and the beaches leading toward Diani, make Mombasa Kenya's most visited coastal tourism gateway. Hotels, tour operators and the informal beach-trade economy (curio sellers, beach boys, water-sports operators) employ tens of thousands, though the sector remains exposed to seasonality and periodic security-driven downturns in visitor numbers.
Housing, informal settlements & coastal flooding
Over a third of Mombasa's population lives in informal settlements in Kisauni, Likoni and Changamwe, many on low-lying or reclaimed land exposed to seasonal flooding, coastal erosion and rising sea levels. Poor drainage and dense construction compound flood risk every long-rains season, while tenure insecurity limits residents' ability to invest in flood-resilient housing.
Transport & governance
The Likoni ferry crossing — one of Africa's busiest, moving hundreds of thousands of passengers daily at peak — and the newer Likoni floating footbridge symbolise Mombasa's chronic congestion between the island CBD and its southern mainland suburbs. As a devolved county hosting national-government port, security and customs functions, Mombasa also navigates recurring national–county jurisdictional friction.

Climate risk context

Mombasa County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

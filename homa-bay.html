Homa Bay County briefing

Kenya · area 3,154 km² · density 359 / km² · avg household 4.4 persons

Homa Bay hugs the eastern shore of Lake Victoria's Winam Gulf, its economy split between lakeside fishing communities on Rusinga, Mfangano and the Gwassi peninsula and inland smallholder farmers growing maize, sorghum and sugarcane across gently rolling hills. Declining Nile perch and tilapia stocks, water hyacinth infestations and overfishing have squeezed the traditional fishing economy even as small-scale gold panning around Ndhiwa offers a risky alternative income. The county has long carried one of Kenya's highest HIV prevalence rates, making health system strength a defining local priority alongside the more familiar rural challenges of land fragmentation, market access and erratic rainfall reliability near the lake basin.

Lake Victoria fisheries
Mbita, Rusinga and Mfangano islands and the Sindo/Gwassi shoreline support Nile perch, tilapia and omena fishing that remains a primary livelihood for tens of thousands of households, despite decades of declining catches from overfishing, illegal gear and water hyacinth infestation choking landing sites and near-shore waters. Fish-processing, cold storage and beach-management-unit governance are the main levers being used to try to stabilise incomes in lakeside wards.
Agriculture & food security
Away from the lake, smallholder farmers grow maize, sorghum, cassava and sugarcane across Kasipul, Kabondo Kasipul, Ndhiwa and Rangwe's rolling hills. Land fragmentation, erratic rainfall and limited market access keep poverty relatively high, and household food security is closely tied to both harvest performance and lake-fishery income where the two overlap.
Health — HIV & maternal care
Homa Bay has long recorded one of Kenya's highest HIV prevalence rates, making health-system strength — testing, ART adherence, maternal and child health services — a defining county priority with a correspondingly large NGO and donor programming footprint, particularly around Homa Bay town, Ndhiwa and Rongo-adjacent trading centres.
Small-scale mining & environment
Artisanal gold panning around Ndhiwa and the Migori border offers a risky but attractive alternative income where farming and fishing returns are thin, though mercury use and unregulated pit mining raise serious health and environmental concerns. Lake water-level changes and hyacinth spread are additional, slower-moving environmental pressures on lakeside livelihoods.

Climate risk context

Homa Bay County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

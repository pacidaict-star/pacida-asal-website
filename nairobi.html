Nairobi County briefing

Kenya · area 696 km² · density 6,247 / km² · avg household 2.9 persons

Kenya's capital and by far its most populous and densely built county — 703 km² holding roughly 1 in 12 Kenyans. Nairobi is East Africa's financial, diplomatic and tech hub (Nairobi Securities Exchange, UN‑Habitat and UNEP headquarters, the "Silicon Savannah" startup scene), sitting at 1,795 m on the edge of the Rift Valley with a mild highland climate. The county's defining tension is spatial: leafy, low‑density suburbs (Karen, Runda, Muthaiga) sit a few kilometres from Africa's densest informal settlements (Kibra, Mathare, Mukuru kwa Njenga), which house over half of Nairobi's residents on a small fraction of its land. Flash flooding, matatu‑dominated gridlock and a fast‑growing youth population are the county's recurring management challenges.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot nationally, but pockets of child malnutrition persist in informal settlements (Kibra, Mathare, Mukuru) well above the county average

Economy & services
East Africa's largest urban economy: finance (NSE, banks, mobile money), the Nairobi‑Nakuru tech and BPO corridor, government and NGO headquarters, and a fast‑growing 'Silicon Savannah' startup scene concentrated around Westlands and Kilimani. Roughly 4 in 10 workers are in informal ('jua kali') trade and services.
Housing & informal settlements
Over half of Nairobi's population lives in informal settlements occupying under 6% of residential land — Kibra, Mathare and Mukuru kwa Njenga are among Africa's largest. Tenure insecurity, structure fires, and periodic demolitions (railway and riparian‑land clearances) are recurring shocks; the Nairobi Metropolitan Services and County housing programmes (affordable housing units in Park Road, Shauri Moyo) are the main policy response.
Water, sanitation & flooding
Piped coverage is patchy in the eastern and informal‑settlement belt; water kiosks and vendors fill the gap at a price premium for the poorest residents. The heavily polluted Nairobi River and undersized drainage make Mathare, Mukuru and South B/South C flood‑prone in every long‑rains season, displacing riverside households almost annually.
Governance & transport
As national capital, Nairobi hosts all arms of national government alongside its own devolved County Government — a dual mandate that has produced recurring friction (including the 2020–22 Nairobi Metropolitan Services takeover of key functions). Matatu‑dominated road transport, chronic congestion, and the BRT/commuter rail (SGR spur, Nairobi Commuter Rail) expansion are the county's biggest infrastructure conversation.

Climate risk context

Nairobi County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

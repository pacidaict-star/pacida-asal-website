Lamu County briefing

Kenya · area 6,273 km² · density 23 / km² · avg household 3.6 persons

Kenya's northernmost coastal county and the cultural heart of the Swahili civilisation: Lamu Old Town, continuously inhabited for over 700 years, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site of coral-stone architecture and donkey-cart streets. Beyond the tourist draw of Lamu Island and Shela beach, the mainland is a mix of mangrove-fringed fishing creeks, the Mpeketoni settlement scheme's maize and coconut farms, and the remote Boni forest bordering Somalia — an area affected by recurrent Al-Shabaab activity that has repeatedly disrupted schooling, trade and humanitarian access in Lamu East. The county is also ground zero for the multi-billion-shilling LAPSSET Lamu Port project, whose planned 32 berths are reshaping the local economy even as fishing and small-scale farming remain the backbone of most households.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 8–14% (Lamu East highest, fishing/pastoral pockets & insecurity-affected areas)

Fisheries & marine economy
Fishing sustains most coastal households — Lamu's mangrove creeks, seagrass beds and offshore waters support artisanal fishers using traditional dhows and traps around Lamu, Kiunga and Faza. Overfishing, mangrove degradation and competition from the new Lamu Port's dredging and shipping traffic are emerging pressures on a fishery that has otherwise changed little in generations.
Tourism & heritage
Lamu Old Town, continuously inhabited for over 700 years, is Kenya's oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlement and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Dhow-building, donkey transport and coral-stone architecture draw a steady, if security-sensitive, tourist trade centred on Lamu Island and Shela beach — a vital income source alongside fishing for much of Lamu West.
Agriculture & the Mpeketoni scheme
The Mpeketoni settlement scheme on the mainland, established in the 1970s, is Lamu's main farming zone — maize, coconut, cashew, mango and cattle. Rainfall is more reliable than in the deep interior ASAL counties but still erratic, and the scheme has repeatedly been a target of insecurity, disrupting harvests and displacing farming households in bad years.
Security & the LAPSSET port frontier
Lamu East (Boni forest, Hindi, Mpeketoni fringe) has suffered recurrent Al-Shabaab attacks and IED incidents since 2011, forcing school closures, curfews and population displacement. At the same time, the county hosts the multi-berth LAPSSET Lamu Port — a multi-billion-shilling national infrastructure project reshaping land values and employment even as security operations continue in the same sub-county.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

West Pokot County briefing

Kenya · area 9,169 km² · density 68 / km² · avg household 5.3 persons

West Pokot rises from the hot, dry Suguta and Karasuk plains on the Uganda border up through the dramatic Cherangany Hills escarpment to cool, well-watered highlands around Kapenguria. The Pokot people combine cattle-and-goat pastoralism in the lowlands (Kacheliba, Alale, Pokot South) with highland maize, wheat and pyrethrum farming near Kapenguria and Sigor. Cross-border cattle raiding with Karamojong communities in Uganda, fuelled by small-arms flows, has made the northern lowlands one of Kenya's more chronically insecure pastoral belts, compounding drought vulnerability. Kapenguria's colonial-era prison, where Jomo Kenyatta was held before independence, is the county's best-known historical landmark.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 14–21% (Kacheliba & Alale worst, cross-border with Karamoja)

Livestock & cross-border raiding
Cattle are central to Pokot identity and wealth. Recurrent raiding with Karamojong communities across the Uganda border around Kacheliba and Alale, fuelled by small-arms proliferation, makes security a precondition for any drought response or livelihood programme in the north.
Highland-lowland duality
Kapenguria and Sigor's highland farms — maize, wheat, pyrethrum — contrast sharply with the arid Kacheliba–Alale lowlands, giving the county one of Kenya's starkest internal development gradients within a single administrative unit.
Water & WASH
Lowland households depend on seasonal rivers such as the Suam and Wei-Wei, shallow wells and water pans. Dry-season trekking in Kacheliba and Alale routinely exceeds 10–15km for people, and considerably further for herds seeking pasture and water together.
Nutrition & education
GAM peaks in the northern lowlands during drought years, while conflict-driven school closures and pastoral mobility depress enrolment in Kacheliba and Alale relative to the more settled highland sub-counties around Kapenguria and Sigor.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

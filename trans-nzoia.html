Trans Nzoia County briefing

Kenya · area 2,469 km² · density 401 / km² · avg household 4.4 persons

Trans Nzoia sits on the fertile northern slopes of Mt Elgon and the Cherangany Hills, and is one of Kenya's most important grain-producing counties — part of the North Rift 'breadbasket' alongside Uasin Gishu and Nakuru. Kitale, a colonial-era agricultural town, anchors a landscape of large-scale maize and wheat farms, dairy cooperatives and smallholder mixed farming, with Mt Elgon's forests feeding rivers that irrigate the plains below. Heavy rainfall (1,000–1,800mm) and rich volcanic soils make the county consistently food-secure in national terms, though land subdivision from former 'White Highlands' settlement schemes, occasional cross-border cattle rustling near Uganda, and maize-price shocks are recurring local concerns.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Grain economy
Trans Nzoia is consistently among Kenya's top three maize-producing counties; large commercial farms around Kwanza and Cherangany and the Kitale NCPB depot set national price benchmarks each harvest. Wheat and sunflower are important secondary commercial crops on the same plains.
Dairy & agro-processing
Cooperative milk-cooling plants and Brookside/KCC collection routes serve a growing dairy sector, alongside emerging dairy-goat uptake on smaller farms. Kitale hosts several maize and wheat millers and agro-input suppliers that serve the wider North Rift region.
Land & settlement history
Subdivided former 'White Highlands' estates and post-independence settlement schemes created a patchwork of very large and very small farms side by side. Land disputes tied to generational subdivision remain a persistent local governance issue, especially around Kiminini and Saboti.
Cross-border security
The Mt Elgon forest belt and the Kenya–Uganda border occasionally see cattle rustling and small-arms flows linked to the wider Sabaot/Karamojong corridor, though at far lower intensity and frequency than in the pastoral north.

Climate risk context

Trans Nzoia County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

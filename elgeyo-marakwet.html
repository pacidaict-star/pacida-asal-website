Elgeyo-Marakwet County briefing

Kenya · area 3,030 km² · density 150 / km² · avg household 4.5 persons

Elgeyo-Marakwet is defined by a single dramatic feature: the Elgeyo Escarpment, which drops nearly 2,000 metres from the cool, tea-and-dairy highlands around Iten and Kapsowar into the hot, dry Kerio Valley floor. Iten itself is globally famous as the 'Home of Champions', a high-altitude training hub that has produced an outsized share of the world's elite distance runners and now anchors a growing sports-tourism economy. Below the escarpment, Marakwet East's Kerio Valley communities farm sorghum, keep livestock and harvest honey in a much drier, more marginal environment prone to Pokot–Marakwet cattle-raiding tension. Few counties compress so much ecological and economic contrast into so short a horizontal distance.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Highland agriculture
The Elgeyo escarpment highlands around Iten, Tambach, Chepkorio and Kapsowar support maize, wheat, dairy and horticulture on fertile, well-watered soils, giving most of the county a genuinely high-potential agricultural profile. Cooperative dairy processing and tea pockets in Keiyo South add further diversification to household incomes above the escarpment.
Athletics economy
Iten's global reputation as the 'Home of Champions' has built a genuine sports-tourism industry: foreign athletes' training camps, guesthouses, physiotherapy clinics and running tours generate income well beyond agriculture, and local prize-money and sponsorship earnings from world-class Kalenjin runners meaningfully supplement household finances across Keiyo North and South.
Kerio Valley drylands
Below the escarpment, Marakwet East's Kerio Valley floor is hot, dry and marginal — sorghum, goats and honey production replace the highland's dairy and horticulture. Recurrent Marakwet–Pokot cattle-raiding along the valley has historically displaced communities and restricted access to grazing and farmland, making peacebuilding as central to local resilience as rainfall.
Escarpment & environment
The near-vertical drop from highland to valley floor makes parts of the county prone to landslides and soil erosion, particularly after heavy rains; the escarpment forests also form a key water catchment feeding the Kerio and, downstream, Lake Turkana basins, giving the county's land-use decisions consequences well beyond its own boundary.

Climate risk context

Elgeyo-Marakwet County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

Kilifi County briefing

Kenya · area 12,246 km² · density 119 / km² · avg household 4.8 persons

Kilifi is Kenya's coast-versus-hinterland ASAL in miniature. Along the Indian Ocean, Malindi's historic Swahili-Portuguese town, Watamu's marine park and the Kilifi Creek beach belt anchor a globally known tourism economy just a few kilometres from the semi-arid Mijikenda hinterland of Ganze and Magarini, where erratic rains regularly push maize and livestock-dependent households into food insecurity. Cashew, coconut and copra have long been the coastal-belt mainstays, though ageing trees and disease have eroded returns. The Arabuko-Sokoke Forest, the largest surviving fragment of East Africa's coastal forest, sits between the two worlds as a globally significant biodiversity reserve. NDMA classifies Kilifi's interior wards among the county's most food-insecure in most years.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 9–15% in drought years (Ganze and Magarini highest)

Coast tourism & fisheries
Malindi, Watamu and the Kilifi Creek beach belt anchor a globally known tourism economy (marine parks, historic Swahili/Portuguese heritage at Gede and Malindi), alongside artisanal fishing along the reef and creek systems — a stark contrast to the arid hinterland a few kilometres inland.
Rain-fed agriculture & tree crops
Cashew nut, coconut/copra and maize are the historic mainstays of the coastal-belt Mijikenda economy, but ageing trees, crop disease and erratic short rains have pushed many households toward casual coastal labour and small trade.
Hinterland food security
Ganze, Magarini and parts of Kaloleni sit in the semi-arid hinterland where maize and livestock depend on increasingly unreliable rains; NDMA classifies these wards among Kilifi's most food-insecure areas most years, with GAM rising sharply after poor seasons.
Water, land & forest
The Arabuko-Sokoke Forest is a globally significant biodiversity site under pressure from charcoal and farmland expansion; countywide, groundwater salinity and long dry-season water-trekking distances remain persistent hinterland challenges.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

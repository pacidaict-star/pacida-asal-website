Busia County briefing

Kenya · area 1,628 km² · density 549 / km² · avg household 4.5 persons

Busia is Kenya's western gateway, its economy built almost entirely around two things: the Malaba and Busia border crossings with Uganda — among the busiest in East Africa — and Lake Victoria, whose Port Victoria and Sio Port landing sites still support a declining but vital fishing industry. Smallholder farmers grow maize, cassava, sorghum and sugarcane across a low, humid plain, while the Budalangi floodplain along the Nzoia River is one of Kenya's most flood-prone landscapes, submerging homes and farms in most rainy seasons. Cross-border informal trade, transport and warehousing employ a large share of residents, and poverty remains among the higher rates in the former Western province.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Cross-border trade
Busia and Malaba are two of Kenya's busiest land border posts with Uganda, and informal and formal trade — food, fuel, manufactured goods, transport and warehousing — is the county's largest employer outside agriculture. Border-post modernisation (one-stop border posts) has improved formal customs flow but done little to reduce dependence on informal cross-border commerce for household income.
Lake Victoria fisheries
Port Victoria, Sio Port and the Budalangi shoreline support a Nile perch, tilapia and omena fishing economy that has weakened over decades due to overfishing, illegal gear and water hyacinth. Declining catches push younger fishers toward casual labour or cross-border trade, while fish-processing and cold-chain investment remain limited.
Flooding
The Budalangi floodplain along the lower Nzoia River is among Kenya's most flood-prone landscapes, submerging homes, roads and cropland in most long-rains seasons and periodically triggering mass displacement. Dykes and early-warning systems have reduced but not eliminated the annual cycle of flood damage, relief response and slow agricultural recovery.
Agriculture & food security
Maize, cassava, sorghum and sugarcane smallholder farming supports most rural households, but poverty remains among the higher rates in the former Western province, driven by small landholdings, market access constraints and periodic flood damage to standing crops. Sugarcane out-grower schemes tied to regional millers provide one of the few reliable cash-crop incomes.

Climate risk context

Busia County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

Nyamira County briefing

Kenya · area 899.7 km² · density 673 / km² · avg household 4.16 persons

Nyamira is one of Kenya's smallest counties by area yet among its most densely populated, a hilly, intensively farmed tea and coffee county in the western highlands bordering Kisii. Generations of subdivision have left many households farming well under half a hectare, concentrating livelihoods on high-value smallholder tea alongside maize, banana and finger millet for subsistence, supplemented by zero-grazing dairy. The resulting land pressure, rather than drought, is Nyamira's defining structural vulnerability, driving persistent poverty and heavy youth out-migration to Kisii, Nairobi and beyond despite the county's strong record on education.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Sector deep-dive click a heading to expand

Tea economy
Nyamira is one of Kenya's most intensively tea-cultivated counties, with several KTDA factories processing smallholder green leaf from Kitutu Masaba, Borabu and West Mugirango; tea income is the backbone of most household budgets.
Land fragmentation & food security
At roughly 670 people per km², Nyamira is among Kenya's most densely settled counties; average landholdings have shrunk to well under half a hectare, squeezing food-crop production (maize, banana, finger millet) and pushing many households toward off-farm income.
Dairy & livelihood diversification
Zero-grazing dairy on small plots supplements tea income and provides year-round cash flow; cooperative milk collection has expanded steadily, though feed and fodder scarcity on tiny landholdings limit herd sizes.
Education & migration
Nyamira has a strong tradition of educational attainment, but land pressure drives significant youth out-migration to Kisii town, Nairobi and other urban centres for work, with remittances an increasingly important household income source.

Climate risk context

Nyamira County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

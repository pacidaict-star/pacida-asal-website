Kericho County briefing

Kenya · area 2,111 km² · density 427 / km² · avg household 4.2 persons

Kericho is the heart of Kenya's tea economy — cool, misty highlands (1,900–2,400 m) blanketed in the manicured green rows of multinational tea estates (Ekaterra/Unilever, James Finlay) alongside tens of thousands of smallholder KTDA growers. This is Kipsigis Kalenjin country, and tea earnings, more than any other single factor, set the rhythm of household income, school fees and county revenue. The South-West Mau forest escarpment forms a critical water tower on the county's western edge, feeding rivers that flow all the way to the Mara and Lake Victoria. Dairy farming and smallholder maize round out a productive, comparatively well-watered agricultural economy, though land subdivision and casual estate-labour precarity are recurring concerns.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Tea economy
Kericho is Kenya's premier tea county, home to multinational estates (Ekaterra/Unilever, James Finlay) alongside tens of thousands of smallholder KTDA growers. Tea earnings anchor household income, school fees and county revenue, but volatile world prices, rising input costs and estate mechanisation are shrinking plantation labour demand.
Dairy & smallholder agriculture
Cool highland climate supports a strong dairy sector alongside tea, maize and vegetables on smallholder plots; the Kipsigis mixed-farming system is among Kenya's most productive per hectare, though land subdivision across generations is steadily shrinking average holdings.
Mau Forest & environment
The South-West Mau forest block forms Kericho's western escarpment and is a critical water tower feeding the Mara and Sondu river systems; encroachment, illegal logging and the politically sensitive Mau evictions remain a live governance and conservation issue.
Land, labour & social services
Tea-estate housing and services historically substituted for county provision in estate areas; outside them, health and water infrastructure lag. Seasonal tea-picking draws migrant labour from Kisii, Nyamira and beyond, with associated housing and welfare pressures.

Climate risk context

Kericho County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.

Embu County briefing

Kenya · area 2,821 km² · density 216 / km² · avg household 3.5 persons

Embu county rises from the eastern slopes of Mt Kenya down through coffee-and-tea highlands around Embu town and Runyenjes to the semi-arid Mbeere plains along the Tana River. The upper county is classic high-potential central Kenya farmland — coffee, tea, macadamia, miraa and dairy support relatively low poverty and dense, well-serviced settlement. Mbeere North and South, by contrast, are genuinely ASAL: thorn-scrub rangeland where sorghum, millet and livestock are the norm and drought bites hard in poor rainfall years, moderated somewhat by the Tana's Kiambere and Masinga hydro-dams that sit within county boundaries. Embu is, in miniature, the same wet-highland-to-dry-lowland gradient found in Meru and Kitui — one county, two climates.

GAM (acute malnutrition): 6–10% in Mbeere during drought years; rest of county not a nutrition hotspot

Highland cash crops
Coffee, tea, macadamia and miraa farming around Embu town, Runyenjes and the Mt Kenya foothills anchor a relatively prosperous agricultural economy, supported by cooperative societies and factory processing. These export cash crops, alongside dairy and horticulture, keep poverty in Manyatta and Runyenjes well below the national average.
Mbeere drylands
Mbeere North and South are Embu's genuine ASAL zone: thorn-scrub rangeland where sorghum, millet, green grams and livestock rearing are the norm, and food insecurity rises sharply in poor rainfall years. Distances to water and markets are far longer here than in the wetter highland sub-counties, despite sharing a single county government.
Water & hydropower
The Tana River's Kiambere and neighbouring Masinga dams sit within or adjoining Embu, feeding national hydropower generation, while irrigation potential along the river remains only partly developed. Mbeere's water access is the county's central climate-adaptation challenge, contrasted with relatively reliable highland rainfall further north; expanding small-scale irrigation off the Tana and its tributaries is a recurring county development priority.
A county of two climates
Embu compresses, within one boundary, the same wet-highland-to-dry-lowland gradient found across central and eastern Kenya's mixed ASAL counties: Manyatta and Runyenjes resemble high-potential Kirinyaga or Murang'a, while Mbeere resembles neighbouring Kitui or Tharaka-Nithi's drier belts. Differentiated, sub-county-specific programming is essential rather than optional here.

Drought history — the recurrence the index is built for

1999–2000
Severe Horn drought; emergency operations across northern Kenya and southern Ethiopia.
2005–06
Failed short rains; major livestock losses in Marsabit and Borena; catalyst period for founding of local NGOs including PACIDA (2008).
2008–09
Back-to-back poor seasons; food-insecure caseload across ASALs peaks above 3.8 million.
2010–11
Horn of Africa famine — the region's worst crisis in 60 years; famine declared in Somalia, Emergency phases across the cross-border cluster.
2016–17
Drought emergency declared in Kenya; GAM breaches 30% in parts of the north; massive livestock deaths in Borena.
2020–23
Five consecutive failed rainy seasons — worst drought in 40 years. 3.3+ million livestock deaths in southern Ethiopia; 4.4 M people on relief in Kenya's ASALs; Borena cattle herds cut by over half.
2023–24
El Niño whiplash: catastrophic floods (Isiolo among worst-hit) immediately after drought — the twin-disaster pattern.
2025–26
Failed 2025 short rains; NDMA moved 12–13 counties to Alert and 4 to Alarm by Feb 2026; 3.3 M food insecure. March 2026 rains brought partial, uneven relief; long-term recovery deficit persists.

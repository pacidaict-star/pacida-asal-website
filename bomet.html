Bomet County briefing

Kenya · area 1,998 km² · density 438 / km² · avg household 4.4 persons

Bomet sits on the fertile South Rift plateau between the Mau forest escarpment and the Kericho tea belt, home to the Kipsigis community and among the country's most productive smallholder tea, dairy and maize country. Green hills terraced with tea bushes surround Bomet town, while Chepalungu's forest-fringe wards border the contested Maasai Mau. The county is also globally known for producing a disproportionate share of Kenya's world-beating long-distance runners, whose training camps and prize-money economy quietly supplement farm incomes. Rainfall is reliable and bimodal, poverty is driven more by land fragmentation and market access than by climate extremes, and tea cooperative payouts remain the single biggest swing factor in household welfare.

GAM (acute malnutrition): Not a nutrition hotspot — standard child health surveillance applies

Tea economy
Smallholder tea is Bomet's economic backbone, delivered through KTDA-managed factories serving tens of thousands of growers across Bomet Central, Bomet East, Konoin and Sotik. Monthly and annual bonus payouts from tea cooperatives are the single biggest determinant of household cash income and school-fee affordability, making global tea prices and factory efficiency matters of direct local concern.
Dairy & mixed farming
Beyond tea, Bomet's cool, well-watered highlands support dairy cattle, maize, beans and increasingly horticulture, giving most households a diversified food and income base rare among Kenya's more marginal counties. Cooperative milk coolers and processors around Bomet and Sotik towns are expanding, though rural feeder-road quality still limits how much of this produce reaches profitable urban markets.
Athletics & sports economy
Bomet, alongside neighbouring Nandi and Uasin Gishu, produces a remarkable share of Kenya's elite long-distance runners; training camps, sponsorship income and prize-money remittances supplement farm earnings in many households, particularly around Bomet Central and Longisa. Local authorities increasingly promote sports tourism and athletics academies as a deliberate economic diversification strategy.
Environment & land
Chepalungu constituency borders the contested Maasai Mau forest, and encroachment, illegal logging and land-use conflict there threaten a water catchment critical to the whole South Rift's rivers. Land fragmentation from generational subdivision is steadily shrinking average farm sizes countywide, a slower-moving but equally consequential threat to household food security than any single rainfall season.

Climate risk context

Bomet County is not one of the counties on NDMA's official ASAL/drought early-warning list, so it does not receive a monthly drought-phase bulletin the way Kenya's 23 arid and semi-arid counties do. The live weather and Need Index above still update in real time — they simply carry less weight from structural drought vulnerability and more from the raw climate signal.
